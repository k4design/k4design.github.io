the four questions

What is waiting on me, and what is already late?

Summary before detail. The numbers you act on, above the lists you work through.

Every surface opens with the same five counters, because these are the five ways creative work goes wrong: something needs your approval, something is past due, something is stuck behind another person, something has gone quiet, and something new has arrived unlooked-at.

The hero counter is the approval queue — the one number where you are the bottleneck, with the oldest waiting item named underneath so it is a task and not a statistic.

ranking

Ranked by urgency, and it shows its reasoning

A ranked list nobody trusts is a list nobody uses, so every card states why it placed where it did: 7d overdue, awaiting your review, untouched 153d. The rank number is on the left; the reasoning is right beside it.

Anything due within two days turns red and breathes — a slow 1.6-second pulse, not a blink, because a card that flashes all day on a screen you keep open becomes wallpaper. With Reduce Motion on it holds the bright end instead: still unmistakable, just still.

Deliverables often live in subtasks, so parents carry their children in a drop-down and count as one project. Four subtasks under one listing should never read as five projects on a plate.

capacity

Who is carrying what

One bar per person, split by the state their work is actually in. Bar length is headcount-relative, so an overloaded designer is visible from across the room, and tapping a name drops their work out beneath it, worst first.

Weekly recurring work distorts a capacity read — three standing deliverables look like three fires. So it gets its own lavender lane behind a toggle: hidden by default, one click to include.

The lane colours were checked, not chosen by eye: every pair measured for contrast under normal vision and all three common colour-blindness simulations. Where two hues came too close, the initials inside each avatar carry the identity instead — colour is only ever the scanning aid.

campaign flights

Paid media, at the edge of a flight

The Mac rail carries a second source entirely: StackAdapt campaigns whose flight ends in the next three days, or ended in the last three. Those are the only two moments that need a decision — a renewal, or a wrap report.

The window is computed in each campaign's own timezone, so a flight ending 23:59 Eastern reads ends today rather than tomorrow. A campaign with any open-ended flight is treated as having no end at all, because reporting an evergreen buy as finished would be a lie.

Search reaches every advertiser in the account, not just the configured ones, and anything pinned stays in the rail regardless of advertiser or date — an explicit watchlist over an automatic window.

precedence

Four ways a project asks to be left out of the numbers

Applied in this order. Each gate is a real label or group on the board, set by the team, not a setting buried in an app.

A dashboard that counts everything counts wrong. Over months these four rules accumulated, each from a specific misread, and they resolve in a fixed order — the earlier gate wins.

  1. 01

    Tagged R&D

    Appears in the R&D view and nowhere else. Exploration shouldn't sit in a pipeline.

  2. 02

    In the Back Burner group

    Its own panel only — no alerts, no capacity, no hygiene. Deliberately shelved, deliberately quiet.

  3. 03

    Marked Cold

    Out of every alert and off everyone's plate, but still counted in board hygiene — a cold listing with no due date is still a data gap. The card says Cold — no alerts so its absence elsewhere isn't a mystery.

  4. 04

    Named like a step

    A name opening with 1) is a step inside a piece of work, not a project. Still listed, never counted — and the card admits it with a step — not counted chip.

two platforms

One payload, two shapes

Both apps are one SwiftUI target reading one endpoint. Every metric is derived server-side in a single module, so the Mac and the phone cannot disagree about how many items are overdue — a class of bug that is invisible until someone reads the two screens side by side.

What differs is navigation, because a 1240-point window and a 390-point screen want opposite things.

Mac

A wide instrument panel

  • Counters, three tabbed views of panelled columns
  • A rail pinned at 25%: campaigns, incoming requests, live activity
  • Draggable border between the feeds; double-click resets
  • ⌘R refresh · ⌘1–⌘4 boards · ⌥⌘1–3 views
  • Sandboxed, outgoing network only

iPhone

A triage surface

  • Four tabs: Today, Queue, Team, Activity
  • Pull to refresh; polling stops when backgrounded
  • Board scope switcher above every view
  • Works over cellular with no Mac involved
  • Access key in the device Keychain

build

How it holds together

The API is a Cloudflare Worker, which is what lets the phone work while travelling with the Mac shut. The monday.com token lives only as a Worker secret — never in either app binary — and the endpoint is gated by a shared key that fails closed: misconfigure it and the API refuses everyone rather than serving the boards to anybody who finds the URL.
